Voice-First Architecture for Zorecho

One of the biggest missing pieces of Zorecho is natural conversation.

The long-term vision has never been for users to operate Zorecho through dozens of menus, forms, and buttons. The vision is that business owners can simply talk to Echo the same way they would talk to a trusted executive assistant.

Voice interaction is not an optional feature. It is a core requirement of the platform.

Objective

Design Zorecho so every major function can be initiated through natural language.

Users should be able to interact with Echo by:

Typing
Voice-to-text
Push-to-talk microphone
Wake phrase (ex: "Hey Echo")

All four methods should enter the exact same AI reasoning pipeline.

There should not be separate "voice features" and "text features." Voice is simply another way of communicating with Echo.

The Echo Command Center

Echo should become the primary interface for operating the business.

Instead of searching through menus, users simply tell Echo what they want.

Examples:

"Hey Echo, create a Facebook campaign for pole barns."

"Echo, answer everyone that commented today."

"Echo, create a Higgsfield video announcing our launch."

"Echo, stop all scheduled posts until I approve them."

"Echo, grow our social media following before launch."

Echo determines the user's intent and routes the work to the proper AI departments.

Intent Recognition

Echo must determine whether the user is giving:

A question
A one-time task
A recurring task
A permanent company instruction
A department instruction
A business objective
An approval
A cancellation
A modification to existing work

Echo should confirm its understanding before executing significant actions.

Persistent Instructions

Not everything the user says should be temporary.

Echo should ask when appropriate:

"Would you like me to remember this as an ongoing instruction?"

Examples:

Always respond professionally.

Never spend more than $500 without approval.

Post three educational videos each week.

Prioritize lead generation over follower growth.

These become company operating instructions available to all appropriate agents.

Department Directives

Echo should also understand requests directed at specific departments.

Examples:

"Tell Nova to post more educational content."

"Tell Forge to use darker cinematic visuals."

"Tell Atlas to pause paid advertising."

Echo routes these requests without requiring the user to navigate department screens.

Conversation Memory

Every conversation should be stored.

Users should be able to:

Continue previous conversations
Search conversations
Convert conversations into permanent instructions
Review completed requests
Hands-Free Business Management

The long-term goal is that a business owner can manage their company while driving, walking a job site, or sitting in a meeting.

Example:

"Hey Echo, what's happened since yesterday?"

Echo provides the morning briefing.

The owner replies:

"Increase our ad budget by 20%."

"Create a video answering yesterday's most common customer question."

"Send me the draft before publishing."

Everything happens through conversation.

Technical Requirements

Design the architecture so future support includes:

Wake-word detection
Streaming speech recognition
Streaming AI responses
Natural interruptions ("Stop", "Wait", "Actually...")
Text-to-speech
Multi-turn conversations
Conversation context
Mobile and desktop voice support

The architecture should support these capabilities even if some are released in later phases.

Design Philosophy

The user should never think:

"Which menu do I open?"

Instead, they should think:

"I'll just ask Echo."

If a task can reasonably be performed through natural conversation, it should be.

The objective is for Echo to become the primary operating system for the business—not merely another feature inside the application.
